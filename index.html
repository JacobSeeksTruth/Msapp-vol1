Hebrew ▾
Genesis 1:1 ▾
בְּרֵאשִׁית בָּרָא אֱלֹהִים אֵת הַשָּׁמַיִם וְאֵת הָאָרֶץ
Bereshit bara Elohim et hashamayim ve-et ha-aretz

Literal: “In beginning created God the heavens and the earth.”
Word Breakdown ▾
  • בְּרֵאשִׁית (bereshit) – in beginning
  • בָּרָא (bara) – he created
  • אֱלֹהִים (Elohim) – God
  • אֵת (et) – (direct object marker)
  • הַשָּׁמַיִם (hashamayim) – the heavens
  • וְאֵת (ve-et) – and (object marker)
  • הָאָרֶץ (ha-aretz) – the earth
Untranslatable Word ▾

When a single word carries so much cultural, emotional, or situational weight that it requires a sentence or an entire paragraph to explain in another language, linguists and translators use a few specific terms to describe what is happening.

Example – אֵת (et): This Hebrew particle has no English equivalent. It marks the definite direct object and simply points to what is being acted upon. Leaving it out changes the grammar of the original.

John 3:16 ▾
Οὕτως γὰρ ἠγάπησεν ὁ Θεὸς τὸν κόσμον, ὥστε τὸν Υἱὸν τὸν μονογενῆ ἔδωκεν, ἵνα πᾶς ὁ πιστεύων εἰς αὐτὸν μὴ ἀπόληται ἀλλ’ ἔχῃ ζωὴν αἰώνιον.
Houtōs gar ēgapēsen ho Theos ton kosmon, hōste ton Huion ton monogenē edōken, hina pas ho pisteuōn eis auton mē apolētai all’ echē zōēn aiōnion.

Literal: “For thus God loved the world, that He gave the only-begotten Son, so that everyone believing in Him should not perish but have eternal life.”
Word Breakdown ▾
  • Οὕτως (houtōs) – thus / in this way
  • γὰρ (gar) – for
  • ἠγάπησεν (ēgapēsen) – he loved (agapaō)
  • ὁ Θεὸς (ho Theos) – God
  • τὸν κόσμον (ton kosmon) – the world
  • ὥστε (hōste) – so that
  • τὸν Υἱὸν τὸν μονογενῆ (ton Huion ton monogenē) – the only-begotten Son
  • ἔδωκεν (edōken) – he gave
  • ἵνα (hina) – in order that
  • πᾶς ὁ πιστεύων (pas ho pisteuōn) – everyone believing
  • εἰς αὐτὸν (eis auton) – in him
  • μὴ ἀπόληται (mē apolētai) – should not perish
  • ἀλλ’ ἔχῃ (all’ echē) – but have
  • ζωὴν αἰώνιον (zōēn aiōnion) – eternal life
Untranslatable Word ▾

When a single word carries so much cultural, emotional, or situational weight that it requires a sentence or an entire paragraph to explain in another language, linguists and translators use a few specific terms to describe what is happening.

Example – ἀγάπη / ἀγαπάω (agapē / agapaō): Often rendered simply “love,” this word describes self-giving, covenantal, deliberate love that seeks the highest good of the other, independent of worthiness or emotion. English “love” is far too broad and thin to carry its full weight.

Walking in the Love of God Through Faithful Obedience

Congratulations on finishing every verse! These verses form a beautiful path of faith.

Devotional

In the beginning God created all things (Genesis 1:1). This same God so loved the world that He gave His only Son so that whoever believes in Him will have eternal life (John 3:16).

We have all sinned and fall short of God’s glory (Romans 3:23). The wages of sin is death, but the free gift of God is eternal life in Christ Jesus (Romans 6:23).

Trust in the Lord with all your heart (Proverbs 3:5). Seek first His kingdom and righteousness (Matthew 6:33). The Lord is your Shepherd; you shall not want (Psalm 23:1).

You can do all things through Christ who strengthens you (Philippians 4:13). And we know that for those who love God, all things work together for good (Romans 8:28).

Receive His love. Follow Jesus in trusting obedience. He will lead, strengthen, and guide you every step of the way.
